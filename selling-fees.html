Trading card selling fees in 2026

What eBay, TCGplayer, Whatnot and Mercari actually take when you sell Pokemon cards, and what lands in your bank afterwards.

Updated July 2026

Most sellers estimate margin against market price. Market price is not what you receive. Between commission, payment processing, the per order fee and shipping, a meaningful share of every sale never reaches you, and the gap is wide enough to turn a flip you were pleased with into one that barely paid for itself.

Here is what each marketplace charges right now, and what it means on real numbers.

The detail that costs people the most

On eBay, the fee applies to the total the buyer pays, including shipping and sales tax. Not the item price. If a card sells for $200 with $5 shipping and $15 of sales tax, the fee is calculated on $220, not $200. At an average 7.5 percent sales tax that is worth about a full percentage point of your real margin, and it is the single most commonly missed cost when sellers do this arithmetic in their head.

TCGplayer changed in February 2026

TCGplayer moved to a 10.75 percent commission for most sellers on February 10, 2026, with payment processing of 2.5 percent plus 30 cents charged separately. Any margin calculation built on the older rate is now understating fees. Commission is capped at $75 per item, which begins to matter above roughly $698 and makes TCGplayer distinctly cheaper on high value singles.

Why this matters for buying

A sealed box at $120 retail with a $160 market price looks like $40 of profit. After eBay fees on $160 you keep about $138, so the real margin is closer to $18 before shipping and before your time. That is the difference between a flip worth chasing and one that is not.

Costs the headline rate leaves out

Shipping

eBay Standard Envelope runs about $1 for raw singles under $20 and is the cheapest realistic option for cards. Anything requiring tracking and a bubble mailer starts near $4 to $5. On a $20 card, shipping can cost more than the marketplace fee.

Promoted listings

Optional on eBay, but competitive categories effectively expect it, and rates of 2 to 10 percent are normal. A seller running 5 percent promoted listings is paying an effective 18 percent, not 13.25, and most people never fold that into their margin estimate.

Returns and chargebacks

Not a line item until it happens, and then it is the whole sale plus shipping both ways. Worth holding a small mental reserve against, particularly on higher value cards.

Which channel to use when

  • Sealed product generally does best on eBay, where the audience is largest, and the rate matches TCGplayer anyway.
  • High value singles favour TCGplayer, because the $75 commission cap makes it materially cheaper above roughly $700.
  • Volume and bulk suit Whatnot, where the effective rate is lowest and live selling moves quantity, if you are willing to be on camera.
  • Cash, in person carries no fee at all, which is exactly why dealer buy rates at shows look the way they do.

Common questions

What does eBay charge to sell trading cards?

13.25 percent plus 30 cents per order, applied to the total the buyer pays including shipping and sales tax. Promoted listings, if used, add 2 to 10 percent on top.

Which marketplace is cheapest?

By headline rate, Whatnot at roughly 11 percent. But TCGplayer is cheapest on singles above about $700 because of its $75 commission cap, and eBay usually returns more on sealed product because it reaches more buyers. Cheapest rate and highest net are not always the same channel.

Do these fees apply to sales tax?

On eBay, yes. It is the most commonly missed cost in seller margin estimates.

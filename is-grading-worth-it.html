Is grading Pokemon cards worth it in 2026?

PSA paused its Value tiers in June, so the cheapest way in is now $79.99 a card. That changes the answer for most of what people were submitting.

Updated July 2026

For years the standard advice was that grading paid for itself on almost anything with a decent chance at a 10. That advice was written when the entry fee was around $25 and turnaround was measured in weeks. Both of those things have changed.

What actually changed

On June 2, 2026, PSA paused all four of its Value service tiers, against a submission backlog reported at roughly 14 million cards. Those tiers were the ones that made bulk and mid value grading viable.

With them gone, the cheapest orderable PSA service is Regular at $79.99 per card, with a $1,500 declared value ceiling and a turnaround around 40 to 50 business days.

The number that decides it

A useful working rule circulating since the pause: a raw modern card wants roughly $150 to $200 of post grade market value before submitting at $79.99 makes sense.

That threshold is not arbitrary. Submitting costs you more than the fee:

  • The grading fee, $79.99 at Regular
  • Shipping both ways, realistically $15 to $30 with tracking and insurance
  • The marketplace fee on the eventual sale, about 13.25 percent on eBay, which on a $250 slab is another $33
  • Grade risk, because a 9 instead of a 10 is common and frequently worth a fraction of the 10 price
  • Time, which is the cost people forget
A worked example

A raw card worth $60. You believe it grades a 10, where it sells for $220. Compare net to net, since selling raw costs you a marketplace fee as well.

Sell it raw. $60 less the eBay fee of $8.25 leaves you $51.75.

It comes back a 10. $220 less the $29.45 eBay fee, the $79.99 grading fee and $22 of shipping leaves $88.56. You are $36.81 ahead.

It comes back a 9 and sells for $95. After the same costs you are at minus $19.88. You spent about $102 to sell a $95 card, and against the $51.75 you would have had by selling raw you are roughly $72 worse off. One grade is the whole decision.

The cost nobody prices: the wait

Forty to fifty business days is two to two and a half months of calendar time, before shipping in either direction. For a collector that is patience. For anyone buying and reselling, it is capital you cannot use.

Money in a submission is money not buying the next drop. If you turn inventory even monthly, a card at PSA has skipped two or three cycles. That opportunity cost rarely appears in grading calculators and it is often larger than the fee.

When it still clearly makes sense

  • High value cards, where the 10 premium is measured in hundreds and the fee is a rounding error
  • Vintage, where authentication matters as much as the grade and raw buyers are rightly cautious
  • Cards you already own with obvious 10 potential, sharp corners, clean surface, good centering, where you are not also paying to acquire
  • Cards you intend to keep, where protection and presentation are the point and resale math is beside it

When it probably does not

  • Bulk, which is what the Value tiers existed for and no longer has an economical route
  • Modern cards under about $150 graded, where fees and grade risk eat the spread
  • Anything you are unsure about on centering, since centering is the most common reason a promising card returns a 9
  • Cards you need liquid, because a two month wait is a two month wait

Other graders

SGC and CGC generally price below PSA, and Beckett offers lower tiers as well. But a cheaper slab is only better if the market pays similarly for it, and for Pokemon the PSA premium is real. The correct comparison is not fee against fee, it is fee against the premium that specific grader earns on that specific card. Sometimes a $79.99 PSA beats a cheaper alternative comfortably. Sometimes neither is worth doing.

Common questions

How much does PSA cost now?

Regular is $79.99 per card with a $1,500 declared value limit and roughly 40 to 50 business days. The Value tiers at $24.99 and $32.99 were paused on June 2, 2026.

What is the break even for grading?

As a rule of thumb, about $150 to $200 of post grade value on a modern card. Below that, fees, shipping, marketplace commission and grade risk usually consume the premium.

Why did PSA pause the Value tiers?

A submission backlog reported around 14 million cards. Pausing the cheapest tiers reduces intake volume.

Is a PSA 9 worth grading for?

Rarely on modern cards. The gap between 9 and 10 prices is usually where the entire economics of the submission lives, which is why honest self assessment of centering and surface matters more than optimism.

Should I just sell raw instead?

Often, yes, especially under the threshold. Selling raw is immediate, carries no grade risk, and frees capital for the next buy. Marketplace fees still apply, so run that number too.
